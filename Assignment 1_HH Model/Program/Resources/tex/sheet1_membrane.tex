\sect{Current balance (Kirchhoff's current law)}
\begin{align}
J_{in} &= J_C + J_{Na} + J_K + J_L,\qquad J_C = C_m\frac{dv}{dt} \\
\Rightarrow\quad &\hlbox{\frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{1}{C_m}\Big[J_{in} - \big(J_{Na}+J_K+J_L\big)\Big]}
\end{align}

\sect{Ionic currents and conductances}
\begin{align}
J_{Na} &= g_{Na}\,(v - v_{Na}), & g_{Na} &= \bar g_{Na}\, m^{3} h \\
J_{K}  &= g_{K}\,(v - v_{K}),   & g_{K}  &= \bar g_{K}\, n^{4} \\
J_{L}  &= g_{L}\,(v - v_{L}),   & g_{L}  &= \bar g_{L} \ \ (\text{constant})
\end{align}

\sect{Sign convention and output alignment}
The model follows Hodgkin and Huxley (1952) and the lecturer's week-9 material: $v$ is the displacement
of the membrane potential from rest, \emph{depolarization is negative}, ionic currents are positive when
inward, $v_{Na}=-115$~mV, $v_K=+12$~mV and $v_L=-10.613$~mV. A negative $J_{in}$ therefore depolarizes the
membrane. The absolute membrane voltage displayed by the program is
\begin{equation}
\hlbox{V_m = -\,(v + 70)\ \text{mV}}\qquad\big(v=0\ \Rightarrow\ V_m=-70\ \text{mV};\ \ v=-100\ \Rightarrow\ V_m=+30\ \text{mV}\big)
\end{equation}

\nomtable{
J_{in} & injected (stimulus) current density; $J_{in}<0$ depolarizes & $\mu$A/cm$^2$ \\
J_{C} & capacitive current density & $\mu$A/cm$^2$ \\
C_m & membrane capacitance per unit area ($=1$) & $\mu$F/cm$^2$ \\
J_{Na},J_K,J_L & sodium, potassium and leakage current densities (inward positive) & $\mu$A/cm$^2$ \\
v & membrane potential displacement from rest (depolarization negative) & mV \\
t & time & ms \\
g_{Na},g_K,g_L & sodium, potassium and leakage conductances & mS/cm$^2$ \\
v_{Na},v_K,v_L & reversal potentials relative to rest ($-115$, $12$, $-10.613$) & mV \\
\bar g_{Na},\bar g_K,\bar g_L & maximal conductances ($120$, $36$, $0.3$) & mS/cm$^2$ \\
m,\ h,\ n & Na activation, Na inactivation and K activation gates & - \\
V_m & absolute membrane potential (program output) & mV \\
}
